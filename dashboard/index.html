Same line, with and without an inline tag

The transcript is identical across each pair. The only difference is whether an inline audio tag like [whispers] or [laughs] sits inside the line. Gemini reads the tag as a delivery instruction, not as a word. All audio is Aoede, rendered through Cloud TTS streaming.

Same line, three voices

Gemini ships 30 prebuilt voices, each with a tonal descriptor. We picked three with distinctly different descriptors and ran the same line through all three. The labels aren't decoration — they correspond to real prosody differences.

Same voice, same line. Direction dialed up step by step.

Director's Notes is Gemini's structured prompt format for shaping delivery. The voice (Charon, "Informative") and the transcript ("Your delivery window is between 4 and 6 PM today.") are identical across all four samples. Only the surrounding Audio Profile, Scene, and Director's Notes sections change — none, a little, a lot, then pushed to the limit. The #### TRANSCRIPT delimiter separates direction from the line to speak; without it, the model can read your stage directions aloud.

What to listen for: pacing slows, pauses lengthen, emphasis lands on different words as direction grows. Bare is generic; minor adds a slight warmth; major makes it sound like a specific person in a specific moment; extreme tips into theatrical. The point isn't that extreme is good — it's that the prompt does meaningful work, and you choose how far to push.

Direction can also add what the transcript doesn't contain

Everything above just shapes delivery. But because Gemini reads the prompt as instructions rather than a phoneme map, you can also ask it to add things the transcript never said — fillers like "um" and "hmm", breaths, brief restarts, hesitations. Same voice (Charon), same transcript ("Your delivery window is between 4 and 6 PM today."). Only the prompt changes.

What to listen for: the bare version reads the line. The fillers version sounds like someone glancing at a screen as they talk — small "um" or "hmm", a breath before the numbers, maybe a tiny self-correction. Words appear that aren't in the transcript. For voice agents, this is the single biggest lever for sounding human instead of TTS.

Consistency. Same input rendered 10 times
Does the model produce the same audio twice for the same prompt? We rendered the same 12-word sentence 10 times and measured two things: total audio duration, and root-mean-square (RMS) loudness. Both are reported as coefficient of variation (CV): the standard deviation divided by the mean.

How to read CV. A CV of 0.029 means the run-to-run variation is about 2.9% of the mean. CV below 0.05 is near-identical output across runs (essentially deterministic). CV between 0.05 and 0.15 is mild variability. Higher than 0.15 means the model is rendering the same input visibly differently each time.

In our test, duration is very stable (CV 0.029). The model decides the same pacing every time. But loudness varies ±11% (CV 0.107). Delivery intensity is not fully deterministic. If you need identical volume across cached prompts, you'd want to normalize loudness in post-processing.
